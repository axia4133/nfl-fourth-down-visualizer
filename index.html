01 / THE QUESTION

Would you make the same call?

Choose a fourth-down situation and compare your own decision against observed NFL behavior.

02 / THE METHOD

Similar situations, not identical plays

The comparison includes regular-season Weeks 1–9 and matches the selected quarter, field position within 12 yards, and distance to go within 2 yards. If fewer than eight plays match, the search expands to ±20 and ±4 yards, always showing the window and sample size.

03 / THE LIMITATION

Descriptive ≠ optimal

These statistics summarize observed choices; they do not estimate win probability, expected points, or the best possible decision. Small samples and missing plays can affect patterns.

INITIAL PROTOTYPE // DSC 106

BUILD NOTES & NEXT STEPS.

Our progress and design challenges for the November 18 prototype milestone.

01 / WHAT HAVE WE DONE SO FAR?

From proposal to interactive experience

We translated our NFL fourth-down proposal into an explorable coaching challenge. We built an interactive football field where visitors can change field position, yards to go, and quarter before choosing a play. We implemented a D3-driven decision chart and field-position heatmap designed to update from real play-by-play records. We tested the parser against the Kaggle 2026 file, excluded preseason and penalty-only rows, and added sample-size notices, labels, and a responsive layout for GitHub Pages.

02 / WHAT WILL BE MOST CHALLENGING?

Making comparisons fair and meaningful

The hardest part will be deciding which fourth-down situations are comparable without hiding important differences in game context. Our Kaggle CSV stores the down and field position inside text rather than separate columns, so we had to parse those values and check them against team abbreviations. Very specific filters may produce small samples that make percentages unstable or misleading. We plan to show the number of matching plays, disclose when the matching window expands, and test the visualizations against the source data before drawing conclusions. We also want to keep the interactive story engaging without implying that the most common choice is automatically the best choice.
